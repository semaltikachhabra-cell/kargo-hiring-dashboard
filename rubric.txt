KARGO HIRING RUBRIC — Product Manager (PM) and Senior Product Manager (SPM)
Derived from the 8 past-hire profiles and their last performance ratings, not from the job descriptions.

Calibration set
  Exceeds Expectations : Rohan Desai, Sunita Krishnamurthy, Aditya Shetty, Meghna Tiwari, Lavanya Iyer
  Meets Expectations   : Vikram Nair, Rahul Bose
  Below Expectations   : Preetham Rao

PATTERNS FOUND
1. They did the operational work with their own hands before moving to a desk role.
   Rohan ran 180+ shipments a month of BoL/customs paperwork at a CHA in JNPT; Sunita handled 200+ shipments a month of
   documentation; Aditya handled berth and detention escalations at a JNPT terminal; Meghna resolved a customs hold
   overnight; Lavanya ran carrier allocation for 800+ shipments a month. Vikram (HR-tech, research done through
   interviews), Rahul (fintech marketing) and Preetham (integrated 3PL APIs but never worked inside an operation) did not.
2. They noticed a gap nobody had asked them to fix, built the fix themselves, and peers adopted it without a mandate.
   Rohan's Excel tracker was adopted by 12 people in two weeks and his weekend BoL prototype had 30 users in a month;
   Sunita rebuilt the intake workflow over a weekend and the team kept it; Aditya's loss post-mortem became standard
   practice; Meghna's 30-60-90 framework is used by the whole CS team; Lavanya's dashboard spread to 2 other teams.
   The lower-rated hires' wins were assigned work done well (Vikram's features, Preetham's indexing work, Rahul's targets).
3. They owned the outcome with no layer above them to catch mistakes.
   Rohan translated field needs into specs "without a product layer"; Aditya ran the full enterprise cycle alone with
   "no account manager layer"; Meghna had no escalation to management in 14 months; Lavanya was sole PM and "doesn't
   hedge". Vikram worked inside a 4-person PM team presenting to the CTO; Preetham was one of 12 engineers on a team.
4. They write down what failed and change how the team works because of it.
   Aditya documented why a 4-month deal was lost and it became qualification practice; Lavanya killed 2 features on
   usage data and wrote the outage post-mortem; Meghna wrote up an undocumented product limitation and ran the fix.
   The Meets/Below profiles list only wins.

------------------------------------------------------------
ROLE: PM
------------------------------------------------------------
Criterion name: Hands-on operations exposure
What a strong candidate looks like: Has personally done operational work in logistics, freight, supply chain or a
similar field operation (handled shipments, documents, carriers, escalations, the floor) rather than only studying it
through interviews or dashboards. Strong = named volumes or situations they handled themselves. Weak = only
desk-based research, or a domain with no operational floor.
Weight: 30
Source: Rohan, Sunita, Aditya, Meghna, Lavanya (Exceeds) vs Vikram, Rahul, Preetham

Criterion name: Self-started fixes that others adopted
What a strong candidate looks like: Spotted a problem nobody assigned to them, built a working fix quickly (a tool,
sheet, process or prototype), and peers or other teams adopted it on their own. Strong = says who adopted it and how
fast. Weak = only delivered assigned features or targets, however well.
Weight: 30
Source: Rohan's tracker and BoL prototype, Sunita's weekend workflow, Meghna's framework, Lavanya's dashboard

Criterion name: Ownership without a safety net
What a strong candidate looks like: Has been the person accountable for an area with nobody senior checking their
calls: sole owner, first hire in a function, ran the full cycle alone, handled crises without escalating. Weak = was
one contributor inside a larger team or hierarchy.
Weight: 20
Source: Rohan, Aditya, Meghna, Lavanya vs Vikram (4-PM team) and Preetham (12-engineer team)

Criterion name: Learns in public from what failed
What a strong candidate looks like: Describes something that did not work (a lost deal, a killed feature, an outage, a
bug) and what they documented or changed because of it. Weak = the CV lists only successes.
Weight: 20
Source: Aditya's lost-deal post-mortem, Lavanya's killed features and outage post-mortem, Meghna's limitation write-up

------------------------------------------------------------
ROLE: SPM
------------------------------------------------------------
Criterion name: Ownership without a safety net
What a strong candidate looks like: Over several years has independently owned a whole area, function or P&L with
no senior layer making the hard calls: built it from zero, was the most senior person in the room, made decisions
and lived with the consequences. The bar is sustained, repeated independent ownership, not one project.
Weight: 30
Source: Rohan ("most senior engineer in the room"), Aditya (full cycle alone), Lavanya (sole PM) vs Vikram, Preetham

Criterion name: Hands-on operations exposure
What a strong candidate looks like: Has personally worked inside a logistics, freight, port or supply chain operation
and can describe specific systems, volumes and breakdowns they handled. Strong = years inside the operation, not a
short stint. Weak = understands the domain only as a vendor or analyst.
Weight: 25
Source: Rohan (CHA at JNPT), Sunita (forwarder documentation), Aditya (JNPT terminal), Lavanya (3PL carrier ops)

Criterion name: Self-started fixes that others adopted
What a strong candidate looks like: Has more than once built something unasked that became the team's or company's
standard way of working (a module, a framework, a practice). For SPM, at least one example should have spread beyond
their own team.
Weight: 25
Source: Rohan's BoL module became a core feature; Lavanya's dashboard spread to 2 teams; Meghna's framework

Criterion name: Learns in public from what failed
What a strong candidate looks like: Has led a post-mortem, killed their own work on evidence, or changed team
practice after a failure, and says so plainly. Weak = only wins listed, or failures blamed on others.
Weight: 20
Source: Aditya, Lavanya, Meghna
